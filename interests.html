Interests

My interests sit somewhere between neuroscience, cognitive science, AI and philosophy. These are a few of the areas I keep coming back to.

Learning & memory

How can a system keep learning without losing what it already knows? I’m interested in continual learning and memory consolidation, and whether ideas from biological memory can help us build AI systems that adapt more reliably.

Prediction & uncertainty

How do we make sense of incomplete or conflicting information? I’m drawn to predictive coding and probabilistic approaches: how much does what we expect shape what we perceive, and when should new evidence change our minds?

Representations & reasoning

I’m interested in what a learned representation lets a system do: recognise something, reason about it, or use it in a new situation. That draws me towards representation learning, neuro-symbolic AI and questions about flexible generalisation.

Philosophy of mind & consciousness

What would it mean for an artificial system to understand something, or to have experiences? I’m interested both in the philosophical questions and in how to make claims about consciousness specific enough to investigate.

AI policy & politics

How should we make decisions about AI when its development and consequences are uncertain? Who gets to shape how these systems are used, who benefits, and who is accountable when things go wrong?

Things I’m thinking about

A few questions I keep coming back to outside my coursework. Some might turn into projects or pieces of writing; others are things I’m still trying to get my head around.

Making sense of a word that doesn’t exist

How does a language model work out what an unfamiliar or made-up word means from the way it’s used? I’m curious about how much comes from context, the shape of the word, or similarities to things it already knows — and whether it can carry that meaning into a different situation.

Talking about experience without the usual vocabulary

What would happen to an AI’s descriptions of its own “internal state” if language about consciousness, feelings and subjective experience were removed or reduced during training? Would it find other ways to describe something similar? Removing that language from training is a different experiment from preventing the model from using it in an answer. Either way, I’d be interested in what changes in its self-descriptions, without assuming those descriptions reveal an experience behind them.

Does the voice change the answer?

If the words stay the same but the speaker’s voice changes, does a voice-based language model respond differently? Can it pick up cues associated with age, gender, accent or ethnicity, and do those cues affect its tone, assumptions or advice? I’m interested in what it seems to infer, how reliable those inferences are, and whether they influence a response even when it never names them. Those impressions could, of course, be wrong.

When is forgetting useful?

We often treat forgetting as something to prevent. But could selective forgetting help a system adapt, avoid interference or stop relying on something that’s no longer true? What would it mean to forget the right things?

Prediction, understanding and getting it right for the wrong reason

Does a representation that supports good prediction also support understanding? I keep wondering what would distinguish a useful shortcut from something that can support reasoning when the situation changes.

How far should we take inspiration from the brain?

Which details of biological learning are worth borrowing for AI, and which matter mainly because brains have to work within biological constraints? I like the comparison, but I’m just as interested in where it stops being helpful.

Making decisions before we have an answer

How should uncertainty about AI consciousness affect policy, if at all? What kinds of evidence would justify changing how we treat a system, and how do we weigh the costs of getting that judgement wrong in either direction?
